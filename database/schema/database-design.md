# Database Design

## Ownership

PostgreSQL stores normalized application state, searchable records, audit information, and references to Fabric transactions. Hyperledger Fabric remains the source of immutable traceability events; transaction reference rows do not replace the ledger.

## Planned dependency order

1. Organizations
2. Users
3. Identity verifications
4. Laboratories
5. Herb batches
6. Quantity events and custody transfers
7. Laboratory reports and processing records
8. Products and product-batch links
9. Recalls
10. Blockchain transaction references
11. Audit and suspicious-event records

## Design rules

- UUID primary keys identify rows; public batch and product identifiers remain unique business keys.
- Foreign keys preserve the chain from organization and user through batch, custody, laboratory, processing, product, and recall records.
- Quantity changes are append-only events rather than destructive overwrites.
- Public traceability is assembled from approved relational fields; private identity material is never part of that response.
- PostgreSQL constraints enforce important invariants such as positive quantities, valid coordinates, unique certificates, and unique product-batch pairs.
- UTC timestamps are generated by the database/application boundary, not supplied as authoritative values by the frontend.
